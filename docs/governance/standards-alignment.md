# Standards Alignment — XAGF v1

XAGF is an internal operating framework. This document records conceptual alignment targets; it is **not** a claim of certification, regulatory approval or complete legal compliance.

## NIST AI RMF

XAGF mirrors the practical lifecycle of:
- governance;
- context/risk mapping;
- measurement/evaluation;
- risk management.

Relevant XAGF components include risk tiers, accountable owners, evaluations, incident handling, evidence and continuous review.

## ISO/IEC 42001

XAGF adopts management-system ideas such as:
- AI policy and objectives;
- roles and responsibilities;
- risk assessment;
- operational controls;
- monitoring and review;
- continuous improvement.

Formal ISO/IEC 42001 certification would require a separate certification process by an accredited body.

## OWASP AI / Agentic Security Guidance

XAGF security controls explicitly address:
- prompt injection;
- sensitive information disclosure;
- supply-chain risk;
- excessive agency;
- unsafe output/tool handling;
- memory/retrieval poisoning;
- privilege and connector abuse.

OWASP guidance evolves; XAGF control mappings should be versioned when formalized.

## EU AI Act

XAGF supports concepts commonly required in risk-based AI governance:
- risk classification;
- human oversight;
- documentation;
- logging/traceability;
- robustness/cybersecurity;
- transparency;
- provider/deployer accountability.

Applicability and legal duties depend on the specific system, role, jurisdiction and date. Legal review is separate from technical governance.

## Secure software and privacy practices

XAGF also integrates:
- secure SDLC;
- least privilege;
- software supply-chain provenance;
- incident response;
- data minimization;
- retention;
- tenant isolation;
- recovery.

## Rule

Alignment is evidence.

A control is not “implemented” because a policy mentions it. It becomes verified only when implementation and test evidence exist.
