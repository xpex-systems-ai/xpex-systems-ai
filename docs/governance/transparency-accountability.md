# Transparency & Accountability Standard

## AI disclosure

Where applicable, users should be informed when they are interacting directly with AI or when material content/actions are AI-generated or AI-assisted.

Public disclosures should be appropriate to context and jurisdiction.

## Provenance

For public or high-impact generated content, preserve provenance where practical:
- generating system;
- date/time;
- human review status;
- source material;
- model/provider class;
- evidence ID.

Machine-readable marking should be supported where legally required or operationally appropriate.

## Public claims

Every material public metric should map to:
`claim → source → evidence → verification date`.

Examples:
- “live” requires runtime evidence;
- “customer” requires customer/contract evidence;
- “revenue” requires confirmed payment evidence;
- “partner” requires partnership evidence;
- “proprietary” requires ownership/IP evidence.

## User-facing confidence

Do not present model confidence or fluent language as certainty.

Interfaces should distinguish:
- verified fact;
- retrieved source;
- model inference;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- automated action;
- human-approved action.

## Accountability

Each production AI system requires:
- business owner;
- technical owner;
- risk owner;
- escalation route.

A model is not an accountable legal or business owner.
