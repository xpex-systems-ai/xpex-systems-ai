# Human Oversight & Decision Rights

## Principle

Human oversight must be meaningful, not ceremonial.

The human reviewer must have enough information, authority and time to stop or alter a material AI-driven action.

## Decision rights

### AI may execute autonomously
When authorized:
- read-only analysis;
- monitoring;
- classification;
- internal reversible drafts;
- bounded tests in isolated environments;
- pre-approved repetitive workflows within defined thresholds.

### Human approval normally required
- production destructive actions;
- primary credential/ownership changes;
- financial transfers or binding purchases outside standing limits;
- legal commitments;
- public claims with material regulatory/reputational impact;
- high-risk personal-data processing;
- deployment into a newly classified high-risk context;
- exceptions to security policy;
- promotion of a system to public VERIFIED_LIVE when unresolved blockers exist.

## Independent verification

For material changes:
```text
AI/Human proposes
      ↓
Executor performs
      ↓
Independent verifier checks evidence
      ↓
Authorized approver promotes or rejects
```

## Override

Humans can:
- pause an agent;
- revoke capability;
- invalidate a result;
- rollback a deployment;
- quarantine evidence;
- escalate an incident.

Override events must be logged.

## Automation bias defense

Interfaces should distinguish:
- model suggestion;
- verified fact;
- inferred result;
- human-approved action;
- automated action.

Confidence scores must not be presented as certainty.
